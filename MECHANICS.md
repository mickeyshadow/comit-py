# MECHANICS — the whole model on one page

The model chooses, for every site and year, how much of each technology to
build and run so that demand is met at least cost. Everything below is the
complete list of mechanics — there are no others.

## Decisions (LP variables)

| Symbol | Meaning |
|---|---|
| N(site, tech, year) | new capacity built |
| A(site, tech, year) | capacity available (built, within lifetime) |
| U(site, tech, year) | capacity used (output produced) |
| M(commodity, year) | imports meeting national demand |
| D(site, tech, year) | usage reduction vs previous year (slack, costed) |

## Rules (constraints)

1. **Capacity accounting**: A = starting stock (decaying linearly over its
   lifetime) + everything built within its lifetime.
2. **Availability**: U ≤ availability_factor × A.
3. **Demand**: each site may serve at most its own demand; nationally,
   domestic production + imports = total demand. A commodity with no row in
   `imports.csv` cannot import — sites must serve it.
4. **Committed builds** (`build_orders.csv`): min/max bounds on N.
5. **Committed closures** (`committed_events.csv`): demand is zero from the
   closure year; the wind-down pays no inertia cost.
6. **Adoption ramps** (`technologies.csv` ramp_limit): national cap on new
   build per technology per year.
7. **Usage inertia**: D ≥ previous use − current use; the first year
   compares against the incumbent's implied baseline (so foresight cannot
   pre-switch to dodge the charge).
8. **Pathway cap** (pathway mode only): national emissions ≤ the cap curve.
   Net-zero caps decline to an explicit residual (default 5%), never to an
   impossible absolute zero.

## Costs (the objective, discounted at the Green Book rate)

1. **Capex**: annualised loan payments (PMT at the sector's hurdle rate,
   `finance.csv`) for the years falling inside the window.
2. **Fixed opex** on available capacity.
3. **Fuel**: use × input intensity × the band price. A band price =
   wholesale + network + levies + margin (`fuel_prices.csv`), each
   component per year, each with per-band multipliers (EII exemptions are
   a component multiplier, not a fudge).
4. **Carbon**: use × released emissions × the traded or untraded price
   (`carbon.csv`) by the site's UK ETS status.
5. **Imports**: volume × (delivered price + CBAM). CBAM = embodied direct
   emissions × traded carbon price × the phase curve (0 before 2027, then
   the free-allocation reduction factor ramping to 1) for covered
   commodities (`imports.csv`, `carbon.csv`).
6. **Inertia**: D × the disruption cost × the site's factor
   (`finance.csv`, `sites.csv`).

## Calibrated behavioural parameters (both fitted to 2021–25 outturn)

| Parameter | Value | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Hurdle rate | 0.20 | backcast dip-rebound saturation (vs 3.5% Green Book) |
| Usage inertia | £36m/PJ × site factor | backcast 2022 dip signature |

## The universe (v2 — all UK industry)

`sites.csv` covers all UK industry, anchored to the DESNZ territorial
industry total (~46.5 Mt in 2025), in five layers marked by the `layer`
column: `ets_named` (top-100 ETS installations, 2025 verified emissions),
`ets_tail` (the remaining ETS installations, per-sector aggregates,
traded), `nontraded_named` (NAEI non-traded point sources ≥10 kt, 2023
vintage scaled to 2025 by crosswalk sector ratios), `nontraded_tail`
(per-sector aggregate), `diffuse` (the remainder to the anchor). A
double-count guard excludes NAEI rows naming parts of ETS complexes;
name collisions (NAEI names point sources by location) are suffixed,
and the loader refuses duplicate site names outright — duplicates
would silently merge LP constraint rows. Reports split by layer, so
the verified core and the estimated layers are never blended invisibly.

## Run modes

- **Forecast** (`run_forecast.py`): no target imposed — the model says what
  happens. 2026–2036 annual.
- **Pathway** (`pathways.py`): an emissions-cap trajectory added as a
  constraint — the model says what meeting the target costs.
- **Hindcast** (`run_backcast.py`): 2021–2025 vs UK ETS outturn — the skill
  score (currently 3.7% indexed MAPE, conditional on known events).
  Deliberately stays on the ETS-verified core (`datasets_backcast/`), not
  the v2 universe: the non-traded and diffuse layers have no annual
  outturn, and scoring against unverifiable layers would dilute the
  number's meaning.

## Where the levers are

All of them are CSVs in `datasets/` (forward) and `datasets_backcast/`
(hindcast), every row carrying source / retrieved / basis, enforced by the
loader. `INPUTS.md` (generated by `scripts/describe_inputs.py`) lists every
current value. There are no drivers in code.

## What the model deliberately does not have (yet)

Three sectors (heat / steel / cement) with v0 emissions-implied site
demand; no H2/CO2 cluster networks or transport costs (hydrogen is a
priced fuel with a ramp, not a geographic system); no MILP lumpiness;
site-level results are meaningful in aggregate, per the degeneracy
analysis in the companion comit-harness repo.
